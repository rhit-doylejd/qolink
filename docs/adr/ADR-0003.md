# ADR-0003: Timestamp Capture Points

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Our protocol needs to capture "nanosecond resolution" hardware timestamps in order to accurately measure the latency of sent packets and prove the latency meets our specified bound. We need to decide where in the gateware these timestamps will be recorded and how they will produce a latency measurement.

## Decision

We decided to capture 4 distinct timestamps:
1. upon queueing data in an input FIFO,
2. upon placed the SFD of a frame on the physical medium,
3. upon recieving the SFD of a frame from the medium, and
4. upon popping data from the receive buffer

We will produce an end-to-end latency measurement by sending Timestamp 1 with the packet as a frame field. The receiving end of the link will store this value and calculate total end-to-end latency by calculating the difference between Timestamp 4 and Timestamp 1.

Each end will also calculate native send/receive latency by calculating the difference between Timestamp 2 and 1 (on the sending side), and Timestamp 4 and 3 (on the receiving side). 

## Consequences

Only one timestamp is sent over the link, which keeps overhead reasonably low but allows us to recover end-to-end latency at the receiving end. 
Calculating native latency at each end may complicate the process of reporting the latency of those steps, but saves overhead, improving the data rate.
